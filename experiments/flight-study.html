DRIFTWORKS / COMBAT RESEARCH

Simulate first.
Watch the interesting fights.

Run reproducible duels without rendering. Compare distinct opponent behaviors under matched conditions, find openings and drawn-out encounters, then inspect any case in the flight lab. This is a screening tool; it measures combat behavior, not how fun a fight feels.

Study design

Opponent styles - experimental

FreeSpace-inspired pursuit: closure control and separate gun/navigation goals. Pursuit + escape: adds a bounded no-progress exit and re-entry. Aggressor: close pressure. Interceptor: cut ahead. Passer: attack, extend, return. Baseline: existing behavior. Styles are not difficulty ranks.

What this report will answer

Do opponents deliver distinct pressure, approaches and disengagements?
Which conditions create long periods without an opening?
Do shields buy maneuvering time, or just longer stalemates?

The default is a 60-duel behavior screen: baseline, FreeSpace-inspired pursuit and pursuit + escape, Hercules on both sides, five geometries and four testing pilots at veteran skill.

Findings & interpretation

    Timeouts are unfinished fights, not kills at the time limit. Kill-time medians include completed fights only. The human simulator is uncalibrated; a high win rate does not establish fairness or fun.

    Opponent behavior signatures

    Grouped alphabetically by style, not ranked by wins. Geometries and applicable seeds are grouped. Win/loss is from the testing pilot's seat. Attack/disengage are time fractions. Gun opening means an in-range firing solution; behind means the rear 98-degree cone of the test pilot. Range and fractions are weighted by observed fight time. These are signatures, not quality rankings.
