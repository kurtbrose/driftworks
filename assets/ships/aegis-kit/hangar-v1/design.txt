Flight operations design
The existing through-bay is approximately 106.2 m fore/aft, 85.5 m across,
and 23.4 m high. The preserved gameplay passage is narrower: 90 x 20 m.
The central flush elevator connects the flight deck to below-deck aircraft
storage. Fore/aft holding stands provide staging and light servicing; the
workshop shutters connect to internal maintenance compartments. Personnel
routes follow the bulkheads, outside the central aircraft corridor.

Launch: raise one aircraft on the lift; move it to an available holding stand
for checks; reserve and clear one mouth; move to the center and launch outward.
A ready aircraft can launch directly from the lift after checks. Only one
movement crosses the central corridor at a time. The opposite mouth stays
clear as an escape route, not a simultaneous opposing traffic lane.

Recovery: suspend launches, reserve one mouth, match carrier velocity and
enter slowly under guidance; settle on the central platform, shut down, then
lower to storage or tow to a holding stand. Clear the deck before reopening
launch operations. Damaged craft need a dedicated recovery cycle rather than
joining outbound traffic. The lift should be interlocked flush while crossing.

Fueling, arming, and major repair belong below deck or behind workshop doors;
the open flight deck is for transfer, final checks, launch, and recovery.
In vacuum, markings are guidance and clearance boundaries, not an aerodynamic
runway. Aircraft use thrusters; no long takeoff roll is implied.

Current gameplay still creates fighters at the bay center, alternates exits,
and replenishes up to eight allies on a three-second launch cadence. This art
does not implement the elevator, taxiing, traffic reservations, or recovery.
A future operations state machine should replace that instantaneous spawn:
stored -> lifting -> staged -> cleared -> departing -> airborne -> recovery
clearance -> approaching -> landed -> lowering -> stored. Launch/recovery
must share one bay reservation; player obstruction must delay clearance.

Rendering
The study bake removes 51 small paint/light/portal blocks (612 triangles),
extracts the existing deck, ceiling, and inward bulkhead faces, and batches
those surfaces by finish. Structural dimensions and articulated parts stay
unchanged. Two immutable textures and three batches replace primitive detail.
The ceiling is neutral paint. No new dynamic lights or per-frame mesh building.
